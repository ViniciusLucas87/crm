A customer who calls, texts, fills out a form, or comes through a referral has not necessarily made a decision yet. They may be comparing schedules, waiting to speak with someone at home, or simply dealing with the rest of their day.

That does not mean the inquiry should sit unanswered. It means the follow-up should have a purpose. A small service business does not need a long automated sequence to handle a new lead well. It needs a clear first response, a couple of useful check-ins when appropriate, and a reliable way to tell what happens next.

## Start by separating a new inquiry from a sent quote

A new inquiry is the beginning of the conversation. You may only know the caller's name, service area, and a short description of the work. The first goal is to make contact and learn whether the job is a fit.

A quote is different. The customer has already discussed the work and received details to consider. That stage calls for a different follow-up. If you have already sent an estimate, use our guide on [following up on quotes without sounding pushy](/blog/how-contractors-can-follow-up-on-quotes-without-sounding-pushy).

Keeping those stages separate helps your team avoid sending a generic reminder when the customer really needs a return call, a scope question answered, or a clear answer that you cannot take the work.

## Give every inquiry an immediate next step

The first response should happen as soon as your team can genuinely make it useful. If you missed the call while working, a short acknowledgement can keep the door open while you are unavailable:

> Hi, this is [Business Name]. Sorry we missed your call. Please reply with your name, what you need help with, and your area. We will get back to you as soon as we can.

Do not say you are reviewing the schedule or sending a technician if nobody has done that work yet. The useful promise is the one you can keep: that the request was received and that someone will respond within a stated, realistic window.

For a fuller set of examples, see [what a contractor should say in a missed-call text](/blog/what-should-a-contractor-say-in-a-missed-call-text). When you do connect, use a simple intake record so the customer does not have to repeat the basics on the next call.

## Use two purposeful follow-ups, not a chase

There is no universal number that fits every job. A leaking pipe, a seasonal landscaping request, and a planned renovation move at different speeds. But for an ordinary new inquiry that has received no response, this is a sensible starting rhythm:

1. **First contact:** Reply or call back within the window your business stated. Ask only for the details you need to decide the next step.
2. **First check-in:** If the customer does not reply, try once more after a reasonable interval for the type of work. Refer to the original request and offer one easy action, such as a callback or a time to discuss the job.
3. **Final close-out:** If there is still no response, send one short final note, then mark the inquiry as inactive unless the customer gave you a different date or asked you to stay in touch.

The important part is that each message changes the situation. Repeating “Just following up” every few days gives the customer no new reason to answer and gives your team no better information.

## Make each message easy to answer

The customer should not need to write a long reply to move the conversation forward. Give them one straightforward choice or question.

For example:

> Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Business Name]. I am following up on your request for [service]. Would you like to set up a quick call to discuss the work?

If you need a scheduling detail, say what it is:

> Hi [Name], we have a couple of openings later this week. If you are still looking for help with [service], reply with a good time to call and we can see whether the work fits.

Only mention an opening if it is real. If you have no current availability to offer, a simple callback invitation is more honest than creating urgency around a schedule.

## Know when to stop and leave the door open

If a customer says they are no longer interested, asks you not to contact them, or has chosen another provider, update the record and stop. Do not treat a new-service inquiry as permission for unrelated promotional messages.

When there has been no reply after your final check-in, close the loop without guilt or pressure:

> Hi [Name], I have not heard back, so I will close this request for now. If you still need help with [service], reply here and we can let you know whether we have availability.

This gives the customer a clear path back and prevents your list from making an old inquiry look active forever.

## Record the follow-up before the day gets busy

The problem is often not the number of messages. It is that a request has no owner, no next action, or no date attached to it. As soon as a new inquiry comes in, record:

* The customer's name and preferred contact method
* What they need and where the work is located
* The last contact made
* One named owner
* The next action and date

Our [contractor lead intake checklist](/blog/contractor-lead-intake-checklist) covers the details worth collecting during the first conversation. If several people handle calls and messages, a shared view can make the owner and next action visible without searching personal phones or inboxes.

Never Miss can handle the first acknowledgement after an eligible unanswered call and keep the callback visible for your team. It does not replace the human decision about whether the work is a fit or when a second follow-up is appropriate. That judgment still belongs to the person who understands the customer and the schedule.

## Review what happens to inquiries that go quiet

Once a month, review a small sample of inquiries marked inactive. Look for patterns: Were callbacks late? Did the first message ask for too much information? Did one service area produce work that your team cannot take? Did customers often need a different time to talk?

That review may show that the best improvement is clearer service-area information, a better missed-call response, or a shorter intake process. The aim is not to contact people more often. It is to make every appropriate contact useful and make sure a genuine inquiry gets a clear answer.
